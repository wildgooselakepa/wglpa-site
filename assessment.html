Lake Improvement Program

Wild Goose Lake Special Assessment District

Wild Goose Lake's path to professional lake care, modeled on North Lake's.

Through the Wild Goose Lake Protection Association, Wild Goose Lake is modeling its lake program on North Lake's, starting with a Special Assessment District like the one North Lake has used since 2008 to pay for professional care of its lake. Under Michigan's Public Act 185 of 1957, Washtenaw County can run a lake improvement program and spread its yearly cost across the properties that benefit, collected as a line on the winter tax bill. The association cannot create or impose an assessment. Only the County can, after a public process in which every owner may speak for or against it.

Our path, following North Lake

  1. Organize. Owners ratify the Bylaws and elect the first Board, including a Weed Control Committee chair, by December 12, 2026.
  2. Listen. Ask every owner, by questionnaire, what concerns them most about the lake, as North Lake did before its first treatments.
  3. Measure. Enroll in the Cooperative Lakes Monitoring Program and start volunteer water testing and plant surveys.
  4. Get the numbers. Ask Washtenaw County for a lake improvement cost estimate and proposed lakefront and lake-access charges.
  5. Owners decide. Send the estimate to every owner, then hold the two-thirds vote the Bylaws require before the association supports a district.
  6. County process. The County holds its public hearings and, if it approves, sets up the district and hires a consultant and a licensed contractor.
  7. Care for the lake. Yearly inspections and treatments, volunteer monitoring, gifts to the Lake Program Fund to lower everyone's charge, and a renewal vote every five years.

How it will work

As at North Lake, the County will estimate the yearly cost of aquatic plant control, water testing and lake management, and divide it among the properties on the assessment roll by tier: lakefront properties pay one rate and lake-access properties a lower one.

Owners decide first

Under our Bylaws, the association cannot support a district, or its renewal, until it has sent every lakefront and lake-access owner, member or not, the County's cost estimate, the proposed charge for each tier and a description of the Neighbor Fund. Owners of two-thirds of all properties on the lake must then approve, at a meeting or by a mail ballot with at least fourteen days to return it. Every owner keeps the right to petition, support or oppose the project before the County.

Our model

North Lake's district

In October 2017, Washtenaw County approved a new five-year term for North Lake's district at the same rates in place since 2008: $220 a year for lakefront owners, $140 for lake-access owners and $70 for North Lake Farms owners. In 2016 and 2017 only half of those rates were charged because of savings in the program.

Much of those savings came from North Lake volunteers who test the water and survey the lake for changes in weed growth. Their work helps the lake and lowers every owner's assessment.

Read North Lake's Special Assessment District page

What a season looks like

At North Lake, the County hires a lake consultant to survey the weeds and a licensed contractor to treat them under state and federal rules. Each spring the County, the consultant, the contractor and the association tour the lake by boat, decide which areas need treatment, and post signs around the lake before treatment begins. Wild Goose Lake will follow the same yearly cycle. Our Weed Control Committee will survey the lake each spring and fall, meet with the County's lake-management staff and lake scientist, and report to the Board and members, and we will post each year's inspection notes and treatment dates in News.

Keeping the cost down

Volunteer monitoring, gifts to the Lake Program Fund and the Neighbor Fund all lower what owners pay. See Fundraising.

Lake studies

What we measure, and why

Good decisions start with good data. These programs track the lake's condition year to year.

Water quality monitoring

Led by our Water Quality Monitoring Committee, volunteers measure water clarity with a Secchi disk, along with temperature, dissolved oxygen and phosphorus, through the open-water season. Results are reported at each annual meeting.

Michigan's statewide volunteer program is the Cooperative Lakes Monitoring Program (CLMP), run by MiCorps. North Lake's volunteers take part, and their records guide the lake's treatments and show long-term trends. The association plans to enroll Wild Goose Lake. More about MiCorps.

Volunteer to be a lake monitor. Readings are taken from a boat or kayak through the summer, and no experience is needed. Tell us you're interested.

Phosphorus and chlorophyll samples, when taken, show how much algae the lake can support.

Aquatic plants and invasive species

Native plants shelter fish and hold the lake bottom in place. Invasive species such as Eurasian watermilfoil and starry stonewort can crowd them out. We survey the lake and coordinate any treatment with state permits.

Our Weed Control Committee surveys the lake each spring and fall and keeps the history of weed control here.
